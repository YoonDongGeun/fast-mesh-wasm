# fast-mesh-wasm showcase

A small mesh tool built on the library: load meshes, move them, repair them, and see how two meshes
touch as a contact heatmap. Vite + React + React Three Fiber over a `SceneStore`
(`fast-mesh-wasm/store`), drawn with `<FastMesh>` (`fast-mesh-wasm/r3f`).

**[Live demo](https://yoondonggeun.github.io/fast-mesh-wasm/)**, published from `main` by
`.github/workflows/pages.yml`. GitHub Pages cannot send the COOP/COEP headers that threads need, so
there a small service worker (`public/coi-sw.js`, registered in `index.html`) adds them and the page
reloads once on the first visit. Where service workers are unavailable the scene runs
single-threaded. The dev and preview servers send the headers themselves, so the worker is not
registered there. `SHOWCASE_BASE=/fast-mesh-wasm/` sets the build's public path (default `/`);
`node scripts/showcase-pages-check.mjs` checks that build in Chrome (see Checks).

```sh
pnpm build                                          # the library (once)
pnpm --filter fast-mesh-wasm-example-showcase dev   # or: build, then preview
```

- **Load.** Open or drop STL (binary and ASCII), OBJ and PLY (ASCII and binary little-endian) files.
  They are read and parsed in a Worker (`src/io/parser.worker.ts`); the page only calls
  `store.addMesh` with the transferred arrays. Each file shows its progress. STL corners with
  bit-identical positions share a vertex.
- **Demo meshes.** A (a blobby sphere), B (a rounded box penetrating A) and C (a shell with two holes
  and a floating piece) are generated in the same Worker when the app opens.
- **Mesh list.** Name, picture, triangle count, visibility, opacity; select, focus the camera, remove.
  A picture is drawn from the mesh's render views by a small software z-buffer
  (`src/features/home/ui/mesh-preview.ts`), in slices of a few milliseconds at idle, and redrawn once edits
  or turns have stopped for 400 ms and no pointer is pressed on the viewport. A mesh with nothing left
  to draw shows an empty state. The built-in models' buttons show committed pictures
  (`public/previews`, about 15 KB in all, regenerated with `node scripts/showcase-previews.mjs --build`
  when a model changes), so showing the list loads no model.
- **Create.** *Create Shape…* on the Home shelf (or *Create* in the command search) opens a dialog over
  the viewport: a box, cylinder, cone, sphere, icosphere, torus, arrow, prism or plane, its
  sizes in the display unit and its resolutions, a name, a centre and a rotation (Euler XYZ degrees,
  as the transform panel). A live preview follows the fields: the shape is generated by the
  engine's `generatePrimitive` in the Worker (`WorkerScene.generatePrimitive`; with `?local`, on the
  main thread) once edits settle for 120 ms, and drawn translucent where it would land. Create adds
  it (the rotation baked in) as one undo step: undo removes the mesh and its row, redo brings both
  back. Defaults are sized to the listed meshes (about their median diameter, 2 mm in an empty
  scene); at most 10 M triangles.
- **Text.** *Create Text…* on the Home shelf, *Text…* on the Mesh Edit shelf (or *Text* in the command
  search) opens a dialog with the text (newlines start new lines), its letter height (a capital's),
  depth and letter spacing in the display unit, and how many straight pieces each outline curve takes.
  Create adds it as a solid mesh in one undo step, as the Create dialog adds a shape. *Emboss on
  Surface* and *Engrave on Surface* arm Place Mesh with the text as its stamp: click a surface to
  raise or cut the letters there, one undo step on that mesh. The Surface Stamp section of the Mesh
  Edit panel offers *Text* as a stamp too, with the same fields, and says how far the settings raise
  it (its depth less *Embed*) or how deep they cut (*Embed*). Text is made on the main thread
  (`src/features/mesh-edit/ux/text-mesh.ts`): each glyph's contours are nested by containment, so
  counters are holes whatever the font's winding; the caps are filled with three.js's ear clipping
  (the engine's polygon triangulation takes holes too, but has no SDK entry point) and walled
  between; and the result is welded, closed and wound outward. A stamp that conforms to the surface is
  also cut so that no edge spans more than an eighth of a letter's height, since conforming moves
  vertices only. The font (`src/features/mesh-edit/fonts`, under its own licence, kept beside it) is
  about 62 KB, 22 KB gzipped, and loads as a chunk of its own when a text tool first opens. It has
  ASCII, Greek and some symbols; a character it lacks is drawn as `?` and named in the dialog.
- **Polylines.** *Keep as Objects* (Section Contours), *Keep contours* (the section plane) and *Keep
  as object* (Surface Path) keep curves as objects of the scene, in world millimetres: they stay when
  the tool, the plane or the mesh goes. They are listed under the meshes with their length, colour,
  visibility, focus and removal, drawn as lines that pointer rays pass through, and saved with the
  project. The Polylines panel (on Mesh Edit and Inspect) works on the selected ones (shift-click
  adds; the only one needs no selecting), each tool one undo step:
  - *Simplify* drops the points a curve does without within a tolerance. *Smooth & Resample* replaces
    it by evenly spaced points of a spline through its points, or through the curve with detail
    shorter than *smooth under* smoothed away (a loop keeps its size).
  - *Offset* adds the curves at a distance from a planar one: outward of a loop, to the left of an open
    curve, negative the other way, with round or sharp corners. A narrow place may split it, a gap may
    close into a hole, and a loop may vanish.
  - *Make Tube* sweeps a circle along each curve into a closed mesh (it says where the curve bends
    tighter than the radius and the tube overlaps itself). *Extrude* makes solids of closed planar
    curves; those of one plane together, one inside another a hole, with the text tool's caps.
  - *SVG* and *DXF* export the selected ones (with none, every visible one) through the contour
    exporter, in their own plane or flattened onto a world one, in millimetres.

  The math is in `src/features/polylines/ux`, all on the main thread and linear or nearly so: a tube
  along 50,000 points and the offset of a loop of 8,000 each take a fraction of a second. An edit is
  an application entry of the scene's undo history (`store.recordEntry`, in `polyline-history.ts`),
  undone and redone in order with every other edit. Display settings (name, colour, visibility) are
  not undo steps.
- **Height maps and reliefs.** The export panel's *Height map* section (Home) makes an orthographic
  depth image of a mesh, or of every visible one, from a standard view or the current one: the
  pixels along its longer side, and the meshes' own height range or a typed one. It is sampled in
  bands of rows, with progress and *Cancel*, and downloads two files: a 16-bit grey PNG, whose code
  is a height (`heightRange * code / 65535`; 0 is also where no surface is seen), and a JSON with
  the pixel size, the height range and where the image's axes and first pixel are in the world.
  *Image to Relief…* (Mesh Edit, the command search, or an image dropped anywhere on the page) does
  the reverse: an image becomes a closed relief with a width, a height for white, a base thickness
  and a number of samples, previewed live and added as one undo step. Picking or dropping the JSON
  along with its image puts the relief on the surface the map saw, within one code's height. A
  16-bit PNG is read at 16 bits (`src/io/png.ts`; a canvas reads 8), other images by the browser,
  in a Worker of their own. The sampling and the relief's mesh run in the engine
  (`Scene.heightMap`, `Scene.addHeightfield`), on the scene's Worker.
- **Move.** Click a mesh to select it; the gizmo (three's `TransformControls`, through
  `attachTransformControls`) moves (<kbd>T</kbd>) or rotates (<kbd>R</kbd>) it, one undo step per drag.
  Undo and Redo, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>.
  They cover added and removed meshes too: undo takes a created mesh, hull, boolean result or copy off
  the list again, and brings a removed one back with its name, colour, place in the list and groups
  (`AppState.follow` keeps the list in step with the store). Opening files and projects is not undone.
  An action larger than the whole undo memory (256 MiB; removing a mesh of about 4M triangles, say)
  is permanent: the status bar says so as it happens (`store.onPermanent`), and Join's report too.
- **Placement.** The Transform panel's helpers place the selected mesh in one click each, one undo
  step each: *Centre to Origin* and *Bottom to Origin* move its world bounding box centre (or the
  centre of its bottom) to the origin; *Lay Flat* takes a clicked face, turns the mesh about its box
  centre so the face points down and rests its lowest point on the floor; *Two Points* and *Face
  Normal* turn a picked direction onto ±X, ±Y or ±Z; *3-2-1* takes three points on the primary plane
  (it becomes the floor, the mesh above it), two along the secondary direction (it becomes +X) and
  one for the origin (its plane becomes X = 0), and puts the datum origin where those planes meet at
  the world origin. *Up* picks the floor: Z = 0 (the default) or Y = 0. *Apply Transform* bakes
  the placement into the vertices (the world positions it gave them), so the placement becomes the
  identity: the placement change and the vertex edit are one history step. The picks are engine
  raycasts on the selected mesh only; <kbd>Esc</kbd> cancels one. The math is in
  `src/features/transform/ux/placement-math.ts`.
- **Build plate.** The Home tab's Build Plate panel (and shelf buttons) draws a grid rectangle on the
  floor: a width along X, a depth along Y, the corner it starts from, and *Up*, as the placement
  helpers have it (Z = 0, or Y = 0 with the depth along Z). They are in the display unit and persist
  with the display settings; the plate takes no pointer rays. *Arrange* packs the footprints of the
  selected meshes, or of every visible one, with at least *Spacing* between them, and rests each on
  the plate with its lowest point, as one undo step. A footprint is the rectangle around the mesh's own
  vertices as it is placed (`src/features/build-plate/ux/footprint.ts`, read in slices: about 10 ms per
  million triangles), so a turned mesh takes what it projects to. The packing
  (`src/features/build-plate/ux/pack.ts`) is deterministic, compact and centred on the plate; it turns
  meshes by 90° only when not everything fits as it is, and then tries several orders and keeps the one
  that places the most. Meshes too big for the plate, or left without room, are named and stay where
  they are, as locked ones do; *Keep clear of other meshes* packs around the visible meshes that are
  not arranged. *Multi-Clone* copies the selected mesh up to 1,000 times, in a grid on the floor or in a
  line along an axis, with a gap between the copies, as one undo step; the mesh and its copies are then
  selected, ready to arrange.
- **View.** The box in the viewport's bottom-right corner (top right on a phone, folded to its toggle
  there at first) holds the view controls. The cube turns with the camera; a face snaps to its view
  (<kbd>1</kbd> front, <kbd>3</kbd> left, <kbd>7</kbd> top, with <kbd>Shift</kbd> the opposite face),
  and the chips beside it to the corner and edge views. The arrows around the cube orbit the view
  90° about the screen's axes (15° with <kbd>Shift</kbd>), so from a face view each press lands on
  the next face view; the curved arrows roll it 90° about the view direction. Moves ease in over
  240 ms (none with reduced motion) and land exactly; a press on the canvas stops one. Under the
  cube: the scale indicator (a ruler, a screen grid of the same step, or none), the XYZ axis basis,
  and perspective or orthographic projection, which persist with the other display settings.
- **Object info.** The box at the bottom of the left sidebar (it folds) reads the selected mesh: its
  triangles, vertices, edges, components, area and volume (`scene.inspect.measure`), average edge
  length, and bounding box along the world's axes or the mesh's own. It draws that box with its
  dimensions, and the face or vertex normals (at most 20,000 lines, a sample on a large mesh). The
  overlays follow a gizmo drag and its undo without being rebuilt. The quality report downloads the
  numbers, the repair analysis and the shown inspect map's readouts as HTML or CSV, in the display
  unit. Past 200,000 triangles the numbers wait until a brush is put down, and the measurement for a
  longer rest, so neither queues ahead of the next stroke. The Angle tool (Inspect) measures the
  angle at the second of three clicked vertices.
- **Repair.** For the selected mesh, `analyze` lists its holes (with their sizes), orientation
  conflicts, components (floating pieces), non-manifold edges and vertices, and degenerate triangles.
  The buttons run the library's repairs through `store.repair`, each one undo step: Weld (within a
  distance), Remove degenerate, Orient, Remove small components (below a triangle count) and Fill
  holes (up to 64 edges by default; an empty limit fills any size, an open mesh's outer boundary
  included; optionally faired to follow the curvature). Each shows its report while the analysis
  refreshes; a repair, its undo and its redo all re-analyse, and an undo or redo clears the report.
  Auto repair runs the library's whole pipeline with those settings as one undo step
  (`store.repair(mesh, { kind: "auto", ... })`). *Denoise* (`scene.denoise`) smooths scan noise and
  keeps edges sharper than an angle; *Delaunay flips* (`scene.delaunayFlips`) improve the triangles
  without moving a vertex, within an angle of the surface. Both run on the whole mesh or on the
  selected faces only, each one undo step, and show the mean normal deviation and the minimum angle
  before and after.
- **Collision.** Pick meshes A and B (the first two meshes by default): each is coloured by its
  signed distance to the other with `contactHeatmap`, penetration from dark red (*Penetration
  depth*) to red, white within ± *Contact tolerance*, and the gap from green to blue up to *Colour
  from*. *Colour from* 0 colours penetration only. Every vertex's distance is exact once the pointer
  stops. While a gizmo drag or a sculpt stroke moves a large mesh (`scene.setFieldInteraction`, about
  8,192 vertices a field per update), the vertices closest to the contact stay exact, a sweep brings the
  others along, and they are all recomputed about 200 ms after the pointer stops or at once on release:
  a few seconds at 1M triangles, in steps, while the page stays live. *Fast mode (large meshes)* bounds the fields at twice the larger of depth and colour from (so the
  coloured area's outer edge still softens with *Boundary softness*), and a deeper
  penetration shows the mesh colour. The colourings are drawn with fragment shading
  (`<FastMesh shading="fragment">`): each pixel is coloured from its interpolated distance, so the
  edge of the coloured area and the contact band are crisp iso-lines rather than blurred across a
  triangle. *Boundary softness* widens them into a blend that many distance units wide (0, the
  default, is a hard edge). Readouts, refreshed at most about four times a second as the
  meshes change, paused during a gizmo drag or a sculpt stroke (the colours stay live; reading field
  values would force stale poses up to date) and refreshed when it ends:
  - **Max penetration**: the deepest negative value of either colouring
    (`store.fetchColoringValues`). In fast mode it is measured within the bound (the panel says
    so), and reads "≥ bound" while the meshes intersect and the deepest value is within 10% of the
    bound, or no vertex inside the other mesh is within it.
  - **Min gap**: `meshDistance`, 0 when the surfaces touch.
  - **Intersecting**: `intersects`.
  - **In contact**: each mesh's triangles within the tolerance of the other
    (`findContacts({ margin: tolerance })`).

  When A or B is open (boundary loops, open chains or open components) or inconsistently oriented
  (orientation conflicts), from `analyze` of it when the pair changes or its triangles change, the
  panel says so: penetration measured to it then follows its surface normals; Orient or Fill holes
  give a closed result. Removing A or B stops the colouring and resets its picker.
- **Collision: Group mode.** Tick several meshes (the first three by default) into a contact group
  (`store.setContactGroup`), measured to each other in *All pairs* or in the pairs ticked in a small
  matrix. Each member gets one field measured to all of its partners and one colour scale, the same
  heatmap and sliders as the pair: each vertex shows its distance to the nearest partner surface (or
  the deepest penetration). Moving a member recomputes only its own field and its partners'. A
  readout per pair, throttled and paused during drags like the pair's: *Min gap* (`meshDistance`),
  *Hit* (`intersects`) and *Max depth*, the deepest negative value of either member at the vertices
  the other is nearest to (`store.fetchColoringValues` and the field's `nearest` buffer, whose
  indices follow `store.coloringTargets`; a vertex inside two partners counts for the deeper one
  only), read "≥ bound" in fast mode under the same rule as the pair. Hovering a member shows which
  partner the vertex under the pointer is nearest to and how far: the vertex is found with the
  engine's BVH `raycast` (one at a time, at most every 50 ms), and the `nearest` buffer is read
  through `fetchFieldNearest`, or `fieldNearest` with `?local`, when the member is first hovered
  and after it changes, never during a drag. Open or inconsistently oriented members get the same
  warning as the pair. Removing a member keeps the others coloured with their remaining partners.
- **Status bar.** The scene's status, the engine's thread count (`WorkerScene.threads`), whether the
  page is cross-origin isolated, and on an error its message with Restart.
- **Units.** The engine is unitless; this app reads its world unit as the millimetre, the unit DICOM
  speaks, and every stored value stays in it. Two separate things convert:
  - *Display unit* (mm or in, the button in the status bar): every length, area and volume readout
    and length input follows, at the factor, its square or its cube. Text only: no mesh, slider
    value or colouring moves. Mesh and section exports (STL, DXF, ...) stay in mm; the collision
    CSV follows, and its header names the unit.
  - *Import scale* (*Assume files in*, or any factor, above the mesh list): a file's numbers are
    multiplied once as it loads, so its mesh is in mm from then on. It applies to the next files
    opened or dropped, shows on the mesh's row, and does not persist. DICOM series and samples
    never take it: their spacing is already in mm.

  The display unit is per project with a global default: a project file stores its own (`units`),
  a file without one takes the default, and picking a unit by hand also sets the default. Opening a
  project never changes the default. The file stores each mesh's positions in mm, with the origin
  they are relative to (a file from before origins were stored opens as it always did, its positions
  taken as they are), and its `importScale` as provenance only, so reopening cannot scale a mesh
  twice. Kept polylines are stored in mm too, and their SVG and DXF files are in mm.
- **Settings.** Display prefs (theme, panel visibility and widths, wireframe, boundary softness,
  repair overlays, collision mode, the last-used heatmap sliders, the default display unit, the view
  box's projection, scale indicator and axis basis) persist in localStorage across
  reloads under versioned `fm.showcase.*` keys; Reset settings in the status bar restores the
  defaults. Meshes and projects stay file-based.

URL options:

| Option | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `?local` | The synchronous `Scene` on the main thread instead of the Worker scene: queries, repairs and `analyze` (the repair panel's, and the collision warnings') then run on the main thread, so they block the page on a large mesh. The exact depth (the default) recomputes every coloured vertex on the main thread on every drag frame, so dragging large meshes stutters: use *Fast mode* there |
| `?big` | Demo meshes A and B at about a million triangles each; `?big=N` at about N |
| `?threads=N` | Ask for N engine threads |
| `?history=N` | Undo memory of N MiB (256 by default). A step larger than all of it cannot be undone; a small N shows what the app then says without a huge mesh |

The dev and preview servers send the cross-origin isolation headers (COOP/COEP, a middleware in
`vite.config.ts`), so the Worker scene runs its multithreaded engine.

## Source layout

`src/features/` has one folder per ribbon area. Each feature has two parts:

- `ui/`: what is shown. React panels and viewport layers, and the three.js objects they draw.
- `ux/`: how it behaves. Input handling, tool state, engine calls and the math behind them. No JSX.

```
src/
  main.tsx       boot: engine, store, React root
  app/           App.tsx; ui/ (the parts that load on demand); ux/ (shortcuts, file drop, loading,
                 display settings, the feature chunks); state/ (app state, tool state, readouts);
                 testing/ (window.__showcase, a chunk of its own)
  shell/         ribbon, status bar, command search, help and tour
  shared/        units, persisted settings, mesh centres, LengthInput, chunks and lazy parts
  viewport/      the canvas (ui/Viewport.tsx); ux/: selection clicks, the sculpt stroke and mask paint
                 drivers, camera and gizmo control, raycasts, the view registry, touch, clip planes
  engine/        the workbench op bridge and the lazy wasm loader
  io/            loading (parser Worker), DICOM, export, project files, demo meshes, 16-bit PNGs and
                 height images (their own Worker)
  features/
    home/        mesh list, project save and open, export (height maps too), the Create dialog
                 (primitive shapes)
    view/        navigation cube (presets, orbit and roll steps, display toggles), shading modes, wireframe
    select/      object and face selection, faces by criteria
    build-plate/ the plate on the floor, arranging footprints on it, multi-clone
    transform/   numeric transform, scale, placement helpers; alignment: ICP, landmark pairs, coarse +
                 ICP, symmetry plane
    inspect/     ruler, angle, query, section plane, clip box, surface fields; deviation, overhang and
                 triangle quality maps; geodesic distance, surface paths, trim and split; object info, box and
                 normals overlays, quality report
    mesh-edit/   boolean (two or N-way), plane cut, offset and shell, extrude (faces, base), convex hull,
                 resample, isotropic remesh, hollow, lattice infill, place mesh, text (its font in
                 fonts/), image to relief
    mesh-repair/ analysis, healer overlays, repairs, auto repair, denoise, Delaunay flips, wrap
    sculpt/      brushes, mask, dyntopo, free-form deformation
    points/      point clouds, primitive fitting, contours, convex hull
    polylines/   contours and paths kept as objects: list, lines, simplify, resample, offset, tube,
                 extrude, SVG and DXF
    ct/          volume rendering, MPR slices
    collision/   contact heatmap, group mode, named groups
```

The page's own chunk (`index`) has what the first frame needs: the shell, the canvas, the mesh list
and the app state. Everything else loads on demand, one chunk per feature:
`features/<area>/ui/<area>-chunk.ts` exports the area's panels, layers and dialogs,
`app/ux/feature-chunks.ts` says when they load, and `app/ui/feature-parts.tsx` wraps them, so that
the shell uses them as if it had imported them.

- **When.** The first screen's chunks load while the engine starts. The others follow one at a time
  once the page is idle and the mesh it boots onto is here. A ribbon tab's load at once when it is
  hovered, focused or opened, and a dialog's when it opens. Every panel is mounted once its chunk is
  here, the hidden ones too.
- **Until then.** A panel's heading holds its place at about its height, a layer draws nothing, and a
  dialog that was opened can be closed. A chunk that does not load (offline, or a newer deploy) says
  so and offers Retry.
- **What must not wait for a chunk** is in `index`: state and its defaults, undo, keyboard shortcuts,
  the command palette's entries. A heavy `ux/` module keeps such parts in a file of its own
  (`align-state.ts` beside `align-tools.ts`, say). A command that presses a panel's control waits for
  the panel (`whenPresent`, `shell/ux/panel-triggers.ts`).

`test/lazy-chunk.test.ts` follows the static imports from `main.tsx` and fails if one brings a
feature chunk back; the build warns when `index` passes 1,000 kB.

Tests live in `test/`.

## Checks

```sh
pnpm --filter fast-mesh-wasm-example-showcase test   # parsers, demo meshes, panel arithmetic (vitest)
node scripts/showcase-check.mjs --threads 4          # a scripted session in headless Chrome (on the build; --build builds first)
node scripts/showcase-check.mjs --big                # plus a 1M-triangle STL upload (main thread < 50 ms)
node scripts/showcase-pages-check.mjs                # the Pages build: no headers, the service worker, the fallback
node scripts/showcase-touch-check.mjs                # phone metrics, real multi-touch input (--shots <dir> saves screenshots)
node scripts/showcase-heatmap-bench.mjs              # frame and colour update times of a drag and a stroke at 100k and 1M
```

The scripted session selects and drags A with the gizmo and undoes it; checks the heatmap of A and
B and its readouts while A is dragged away; picks the open mesh C as B and expects the open-mesh
warning; fills C's holes while it is coloured and undoes it; colours A, B and C as a contact group
(colours on all three, the per-pair readouts, the hover tip, the pair matrix) and removes C from it;
removes B while it is coloured; loads STL, OBJ and PLY files (and a broken one); drives the units
(inch cubes through the import scale, readouts in both units, a project round trip); then repeats a
short session with `?local` (the group's readouts included). It fails on any console error.

The mask session (`scripts/showcase-mask-check.mjs`, `--big` adds the cost at 1M triangles) compares
screenshots: the tint appears where painted, follows invert, hide and clear, reads on light and dark
meshes, and leaves a heatmap's pixels alone. A dab on the mask's edge moves free vertices only and
leaves masked positions byte-identical. The optional stroke-end remesh stands down where it could reach the mask; live splits run over it, and a split between masked vertices joins the mask.

The touch session runs with phone metrics and multi-touch input through `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`,
and asserts on the camera: one finger orbits, two pinch and pan, a second finger never starts a tool
(gizmo, slice and section handles), a tap selects and shows the hover tip in Group mode, panels stay
reachable in portrait, landscape and on a tablet, and a mouse still behaves as before. Sculpting and
mask paint are mouse tools for now: their panel is hidden on a coarse pointer.
