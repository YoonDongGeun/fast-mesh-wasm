- [#291](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/pull/291) [`d98ea13`](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/commit/d98ea135a99ef52fe57dc769d029cbfe70bd41cf) Thanks [@YoonDongGeun](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun)! - Repairs keep the cavities of hollow solids ([#286](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/286), [#283](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/283)). A cavity is a closed component wound inward inside the material of another; `MeshComponent.cavity`, from `Scene.analyze`, tells which ones are. `orient` turned cavities outward, so that a void became overlapping material, and `removeSmallComponents` and `autoRepair` deleted small ones, so that the void was filled. Now `orient` keeps a cavity facing inward (and turns an inside-out hollow solid as a whole), and `removeSmallComponents` and `autoRepair` keep the cavities of the components they keep. Pass `keepCavities: false` to `removeSmallComponents` or `repair.clean` to remove small cavities as before.

- [#270](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/pull/270) [`2647422`](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/commit/2647422c4f7a5911a15c06714481a58b9c02a4fe) Thanks [@YoonDongGeun](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun)! - Add exact 3D convex hulls ([#261](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/261)): `Scene.convexHull(meshes)` for one mesh or several, and `Scene.convexHullOfPoints(points)` for a raw point cloud (flat positions or a `PackedPointCloud`), on `Scene` and `WorkerScene`. The hull is a quickhull on exact orientation predicates, so it is always convex, closed and outward-wound with every input point inside or on it; duplicate, coplanar and collinear points are fine, and a point set without volume is an `INVALID_ARGUMENT` error that says whether it is coincident, collinear or coplanar. The hull is added as a new mesh, filled as one undo step.

- [#275](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/pull/275) [`80f81f0`](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/commit/80f81f0a3244632e74ec90eb254368cdd8e9d922) Thanks [@YoonDongGeun](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun)! - Add feature-preserving denoising and Delaunay edge flips ([#264](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/264)), on `Scene` and `WorkerScene`, each one undo step on a whole mesh or on a face region (`region`, triangle ids). `denoise(mesh, { iterations, strength, featureAngle })` smooths scan noise by bilateral normal filtering and a vertex update: flat faces become flat again and edges sharper than the feature angle stay sharp. `delaunayFlips(mesh, { dihedralLimit })` flips edges towards a Delaunay triangulation without moving a vertex; no new triangle turns further than the limit from the triangles it replaces, edges sharper than it never flip, and the smallest angle never drops. Both reports carry the surface's readouts before and after (smallest corner angle, mean normal deviation). The Rust ops are `fast_mesh_ops::denoise` and `fast_mesh_ops::delaunay_flips`, parallel on the threaded build with results bit-identical to the single-threaded one.

- [#275](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/pull/275) [`80f81f0`](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/commit/80f81f0a3244632e74ec90eb254368cdd8e9d922) Thanks [@YoonDongGeun](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun)! - Add extrusions ([#262](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/262)), on `Scene` and `WorkerScene`, each one undo step. `extrudeFaces(mesh, triangles, { distance, direction })` moves a face selection along each region's average normal, each vertex's average normal or a world direction, and builds side walls along the selection's boundary: holes, several regions and regions that meet at a vertex are handled, and a closed, consistently wound mesh stays so. `extrudeBase(mesh, { height | offset, direction, loops })` runs an open mesh's boundary loops to a flat plane and caps them, turning a surface into a solid that faces outward; outlines nested on the plane become holes. Both report the self-intersections of the result (`selfIntersections`) instead of failing.

- [#291](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/pull/291) [`d98ea13`](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/commit/d98ea135a99ef52fe57dc769d029cbfe70bd41cf) Thanks [@YoonDongGeun](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun)! - Full precision far from the world origin in the remaining ops ([#280](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/280)). `sliceContours`, `sliceContourStack`, `fitPrimitive` and `shortestSurfacePath`, and on a mesh scaled unevenly `trimByCurve`, `selectFacesInSphere`, `selectByCreaseAngle` and `selectSmallFaceIslands`, worked on `f32` world positions, which are off by 0.004 at 1e5; they now keep the mesh's `f64` origin, so that they do the same there as near the world origin. Results near that stay as they were.
  
  - A contour loop has its points in `f64` too, as `points3d64`; `points3d` stays a `Float32Array`. `sliceContoursExport` writes the former. The point a plane is given by may be anywhere on it, however far from the mesh.
  - A `PackedPointCloud` can have an `origin`, the world point its `f32` positions are relative to: `packPointCloud` takes one, and `parsePointCloudText` and `parsePointCloudBytes` read the positions in `f64` and find one with `{ center: true }`. The filters keep it, `unpackPointCloud` returns it, and `reconstructPointCloud`, `convexHullOfPoints` and the `cameraHint` of `pointCloudEstimateNormals` count it in. Clouds without one are `f32` world positions, as before.
  - `placeMesh` takes `stampAnchor`, the point of the stamp that goes to the hit point. It defaults to the zero of the stamp's own coordinates, as before; a stamp more than 1024 times its size away from that, which used to be placed that far off the hit point, now stands on it with the middle of its underside.

- [#62](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/pull/62) [`907f921`](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/commit/907f92189b14a590b3631d2497ee7410bb0f62bd) Thanks [@YoonDongGeun](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun)! - The Worker scene no longer blocks its queue for the whole of a long engine call ([#48](https://github.com/YoonDongGeun/fast-mesh-wasm/issues/48)). The engine computes the same results, bit for bit. What changes is latency, and when a large new mesh's buffers reach the main thread.
  
  - **Field builds and field updates run in steps.** This covers the targets' sign data, the near search and the values.
    - The Worker yields every few milliseconds, so batches sent meanwhile are received and coalesced.
    - A field add for a display that is already superseded is dropped mid-build, as it was before the build started.
    - Longest Worker block at 1M triangles: 118 ms to 6 ms for a field build, and 97 ms to 5 ms for an edit with a field measured to the edited mesh. At 10M: 1.4 to 3.4 s to 14 to 18 ms.
  - **A large mesh is added in the background (a change of reply order).** From 65,536 triangles, its BVH is built in steps while later requests that do not need it run. A toggle sent right after a 1M `addMesh` is answered in about 8 ms instead of 1.18 s, and after a 10M add in 71 ms instead of 16 s.
    - These requests still wait for the new mesh: requests that name it (or a field not built yet that is measured on or to it), any add or removal of a mesh, and queries of every mesh (`raycast`, `closestPoint` without a mesh, `findContacts` without pairs). They then finish it first: in one call on a threaded engine, in steps (still yielding) on a single-threaded one. So the engine's state is the same as in arrival order.
    - Replies can now come in a different order. A reply to an unrelated request sent after the add can arrive before the mesh. The mesh's `mesh:<id>` status reads `computing` and its views are empty until it lands. Its buffers arrive with that status turning `ready`: with a later result, or on their own if the Worker is idle.
    - New `WorkerSceneOptions.backgroundAddTriangles` sets the threshold. `Infinity` turns background adds off, and every add then comes back with its own batch's result.
  - **Field add errors.** A field add now fails, and the field is not kept, when computing its first values fails (out of memory). Before, the add succeeded, the field stayed registered but stale, and the error only showed later. The same applies to a field replayed by `restart()`. A field whose deferred build fails (its name is taken by then, say) now stays known: the error is reported each time it is needed, and removing it works.
