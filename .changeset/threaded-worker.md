Multithreaded Worker scene: on a cross-origin isolated page (COOP `same-origin` and COEP `require-corp` headers), `fast-mesh-wasm/worker` now runs a threaded build of the engine, its WebAssembly on a pool of Workers sharing one memory, with results bit-identical to the single-threaded engine. On a wide-range distance-field drag of two 1M-triangle meshes, 8 threads make a recompute about 1.6 to 4.5 times faster and halve the longest Worker block (headless Chrome). New option `createWorkerScene({ threads })`: the pool size, by default the logical cores at most 8, clamped to 1 to 64; `threads: 1` runs the single-threaded engine and never fetches the threaded files. New getter `WorkerScene.threads` tells how many threads the engine computes with. On a page that is not isolated, or when the threaded engine cannot start (import, instantiation, shared memory, or a thread pool that fails or takes over 10 s), the scene runs single-threaded, and development builds (or the `debug` option) warn once per scene why. Bundlers need no setting beyond the headers; they emit the threaded chunk and its `.wasm` (about 976 KB, 370 KB gzipped) into the build output, which single-threaded pages never download. See "Multithreading (COOP/COEP)" in the README for the header setup in Vite, webpack-dev-server, nginx and Express.
